- 2026-10-05: For nano-vllm [#190](https://github.com/GeeeekExplorer/nano-vllm/issues/190), looked inside the CUDA graphs nano-vllm records for decode, by dumping them with CUDA graph debug mode. The graph for batch size 1 has 395 nodes, 14 kernels per layer, and I could map each one to a step in the model. The graph for batch size 16 uses different kernels, not just bigger launches: matrix multiplies switch from matrix-vector to matrix-matrix kernels, and attention is no longer split into chunks. That is why nano-vllm records one graph per batch size. I also drew how the pieces connect: each recorded node points to a kernel and holds the addresses of the buffers it reads, which is why every step copies its inputs into fixed buffers, and why a table one column too wide breaks the copy. Then I quizzed myself on it. The gaps were about addresses (why the inputs must be copied into the buffers, and what `block_tables` actually holds), how empty seats avoid doing harm, and the fact that one long sequence fails the whole batch. I wrote six harder questions to work through next. Write-up: [notes/nano-vllm/issues/190](../../notes/nano-vllm/issues/190-cuda-graph-block-tables.md#inside-a-recorded-graph).
