- 2026-10-12: Compared nano-vllm with mini-sglang by walking the same example through both: 1000 requests of 500 + 300 tokens, with room for 262,144 tokens of KV cache. The step loop is the same: prefill first, one kind of step at a time, K/V written before attention reads it, CUDA graph for decode. Four design choices differ, and they hang together. nano-vllm keeps 256 tokens per block and shares prefixes by hashing full blocks; mini-sglang keeps one token per slot, so its radix tree can share a prefix of any length, at the cost of a new slot for every request on every decode step and a slot table that lives on the GPU. nano-vllm admits a request with blocks for its prompt only and preempts when the cache runs out; mini-sglang reserves the prompt plus `max_tokens` at admission and never preempts, so in this example it is limited by the 256 rows of its page table, and with my laptop's 47,008 slots only 58 requests would fit at once. nano-vllm waits for each step's tokens before scheduling the next one; mini-sglang launches the next step first. And nano-vllm is one program, while mini-sglang is a server with three kinds of processes that streams tokens back. Q&A in Chinese, the same page in both folders: [mini-sglang](../../notes/mini-sglang/QA-vs-nano-vllm.md), [nano-vllm](../../notes/nano-vllm/QA-vs-mini-sglang.md).
- 2026-10-13: Before starting SGLang, compared it with mini-sglang, with nano-vllm alongside, on SGLang's main branch (`e9400c5`). mini-sglang keeps SGLang's skeleton: the same request path, the same split into processes, and the same four core mechanisms. It simplifies in two ways. On the core path, each step keeps only the simplest policy: first come, first served; one token per KV slot; one kind of KV pool; and a full reservation of `max_tokens` at admission, where SGLang counts running requests at a fraction of their remaining tokens that shrinks over time, and retracts the requests with the least output when that estimate turns out too low. And whole features are left out: quantization, speculative decoding, structured output, LoRA, multimodal models, PD disaggregation, hierarchical caching, data and expert parallelism, and RL weight updates. SGLang's runtime is about 108 times larger and has 527 server arguments to mini-sglang's 20. One finding ties back to my backlog: after inserting a prompt into the radix tree, SGLang writes the tree's KV indices back into the request's own table, which is exactly the step mini-sglang misses (A1). The page ends with a map from each mini-sglang file to the SGLang file to read next. Q&A in Chinese: [notes/sglang/QA-vs-mini-sglang.md](../../notes/sglang/QA-vs-mini-sglang.md).

- 2026-10-14: Tested nano-vllm PR [#281](https://github.com/GeeeekExplorer/nano-vllm/pull/281), INT8 weight-only (W8A16) quantization with its own Triton kernel, which reports +7.3% throughput on an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU. On my RTX 4060 Laptop GPU it runs, its kernel is as accurate as bf16 `F.linear`, and it saves 413 MiB of weights, which nano-vllm turns into 17 more KV blocks (163 to 180); the description's "1.5 GB to 0.75 GB" is off, since the linear weights are 840 MiB in bf16. On the PR's own benchmark it is not measurably faster: 43.30 s against 43.98 s, mean of three runs, inside the 3% noise. Timing every step showed why. Decode gets 2.2 s faster, mostly because the extra blocks cut preemptions from 34 to 24 and the same tokens take 17% fewer steps; per step, int8 is only faster at small batch sizes. Prefill gets 1.5 s slower, almost all of it in the first two steps of about 16,000 tokens each, where the kernel runs at half of cuBLAS's speed. Measured alone, the kernel is 1.3x faster than cuBLAS across all linear layers at one token per step, but two of the four layers (`o_proj`, `down_proj`) get no faster, because 16 thread blocks leave a third of the GPU's 24 SMs idle. Handing large prefill steps to cuBLAS would likely make the PR about 5.5% faster than main here (estimated, not tested). Write-up: [notes/nano-vllm/issues/281](../../notes/nano-vllm/issues/281-int8-weight-only-quantization.md), experiment: [002](../../experiments/002-int8-weight-only-pr281/), Q&A in Chinese: [notes/nano-vllm/QA-quantization.md](../../notes/nano-vllm/QA-quantization.md).
- 2026-10-14: Reading that kernel showed I lacked the basics, so I wrote them down before going further: how my GPU is built (24 SMs, each with 4 Tensor Cores, 65,536 registers and about 100 KB of shared memory; 32 MiB of L2; 8 GB of memory at 256 GB/s in theory and about 196 GB/s measured), how threads, warps and blocks map onto it, and the rules for writing a kernel that follow from that. Then Triton: the common operations and the launch syntax, and what the PR's kernel compiles to on this GPU. Its `tl.load` becomes asynchronous copies into shared memory, its `tl.dot` becomes Tensor Core `mma.sync` instructions, and only 7 of its decode-sized programs (2 of its prefill-sized ones) fit on an SM at once. That explains its speed: a narrow layer at one token per step launches 16 programs, which leaves 8 SMs idle and too few warps on the rest to hide memory latency.
